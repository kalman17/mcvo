\chapter{\abstractname}

Recovering camera intrinsics and extrinsics from casual, uncalibrated video is a prerequisite for 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and augmented reality. Self-supervised methods such as AnyCam predict camera poses from unlabelled video using flow reprojection losses, but handle calibration through an expensive 32-candidate focal length selection procedure that lacks temporal consistency. Standalone calibration methods such as AnyCalib provide accurate single-view predictions but process each frame independently.

This thesis integrates these complementary approaches by replacing AnyCam's candidate-based calibration with direct AnyCalib predictions and introducing a Feature Aggregation Transformer (FAT) that aggregates multi-frame spatial features before decoding, preserving geometric structure lost in simple averaging. Multi-frame temporal consistency is enforced efficiently through flow composition, which derives long-range correspondences from consecutive precomputed flows, reducing the required flow computations from quadratic to linear in the number of frames. A staged training pipeline progressively trains the pose head, the FAT calibration module, and the full end-to-end system using a combination of self-supervised flow reprojection and pseudo ground-truth calibration anchoring.

Evaluation on ScanNet, TUM-RGBD, and Lightspeed demonstrates that the proposed method achieves improved pose accuracy compared to AnyCam and more accurate and consistent calibration compared to AnyCalib, while satisfying all key requirements for learning from casual video: label-free training, feed-forward inference, multi-frame consistency, dynamic scene robustness, and model-agnostic calibration.
