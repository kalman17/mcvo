\chapter{\abstractname}

Recovering camera intrinsics and extrinsics from casual, uncalibrated video is a prerequisite for 3D reconstruction, novel view synthesis, and augmented reality. Self-supervised methods such as AnyCam predict camera poses from unlabelled video using flow reprojection losses, but handle calibration through an expensive 32-candidate focal length selection procedure that lacks temporal consistency. Standalone calibration methods such as AnyCalib provide accurate single-view predictions but process each frame independently.

This thesis integrates these complementary approaches by replacing AnyCam's candidate-based calibration with direct AnyCalib predictions and introducing a Multi-Frame Calibration Transformer (MCT) that aggregates multi-frame spatial features before decoding, preserving geometric structure lost in simple averaging. Multi-frame temporal consistency is enforced efficiently through flow composition, which derives long-range correspondences from consecutive precomputed flows, reducing the required flow computations from quadratic to linear in the number of frames. A staged training pipeline progressively trains the pose head, the calibration transformer, and the full end-to-end system using a combination of self-supervised flow reprojection and pseudo ground-truth calibration anchoring.

Evaluation on MPI Sintel, TUM-RGBD, and KITTI demonstrates that the proposed method achieves improved pose accuracy compared to AnyCam (up to 31.7\% rotation error reduction) and more accurate calibration compared to AnyCalib (up to 32.5\% MAPE improvement), while satisfying all key requirements for learning from casual video: label-free training, feed-forward inference, multi-frame consistency, dynamic scene robustness, and model-agnostic calibration.
