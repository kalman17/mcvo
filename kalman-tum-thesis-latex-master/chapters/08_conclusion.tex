\chapter{Conclusion}\label{chapter:conclusion}

This thesis proposed a framework for fusing pretrained models for camera geometry estimation from casual, unlabelled video. The core contribution is the Multi-Frame Calibration Transformer (MCT), a lightweight module that aggregates spatial features from a pretrained calibration backbone across video frames via cross-frame self-attention, enforcing calibration consistency at the feature level before decoding. The MCT preserves geometric structure that would be lost in scalar averaging, producing temporally consistent intrinsic estimates from a single frozen calibration network.

The framework was demonstrated by fusing AnyCam (a self-supervised pose estimator) with AnyCalib (a single-image calibration network). The MCT is inserted between AnyCalib's frozen backbone and decoder, and the resulting calibration is injected into AnyCam's pose head via a learned focal embedding. Training is fully self-supervised, with only 7.5\% of total parameters trained while all pretrained components remain frozen.

Evaluation on MPI Sintel, TUM-RGBD, and KITTI demonstrated that the fused system improves upon both individual models: translation direction error decreased by up to 39.5\% over AnyCam, and calibration MAPE decreased by up to 32.5\% over per-frame AnyCalib averaging. These results confirm that feature-level multi-frame aggregation and model fusion effectively leverage the complementary strengths of specialised pretrained networks.

The approach provides a general template for integrating pretrained vision models: keep large backbones frozen, introduce lightweight trainable modules at the feature level, and train them with self-supervised objectives. While demonstrated on a specific pair of models, the MCT architecture is not tied to AnyCam or AnyCalib---it can be inserted between any backbone and decoder that share compatible spatial feature maps.
