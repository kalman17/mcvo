\chapter{Evaluation}\label{chapter:evaluation}

This chapter evaluates the proposed framework, instantiated with AnyCam and AnyCalib, on pose error and calibration error across three datasets. The baselines are the individual pretrained models: AnyCam (pose) and AnyCalib (calibration, per-frame averaging).

\section{Metrics}\label{sec:eval-metrics}

\subsection{Pose Metrics}\label{sec:pose-metrics}

\subsubsection{Rotation Error (SO(3) Geodesic Distance)}

Rotation error is the geodesic distance on SO(3) between predicted and ground truth rotation matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{rot}} = \arccos\left(\frac{\text{trace}(\mathbf{R}_{\text{pred}}^\top \mathbf{R}_{\text{gt}}) - 1}{2}\right) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}
\end{equation}
Both mean and median errors are reported.

\subsubsection{Translation Direction Error}

The angular difference between predicted and ground truth translation vectors:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_{\text{trans}} = \arccos\left(\frac{\mathbf{t}_{\text{pred}} \cdot \mathbf{t}_{\text{gt}}}{\|\mathbf{t}_{\text{pred}}\| \|\mathbf{t}_{\text{gt}}\|}\right) \cdot \frac{180}{\pi}
\end{equation}
This metric is scale-invariant: it measures the angle between translation directions regardless of magnitude, which is appropriate for monocular systems where the translation vectors are normalised.

\subsubsection{Average Trajectory Error (ATE)}

ATE quantifies global trajectory drift. Predicted and ground truth trajectories are aligned using Procrustes alignment (SE(3) + scale) via the evo library~\parencite{gruber2017evo}, then the Euclidean distance between aligned camera positions is computed at each frame.

\subsection{Calibration Metrics}\label{sec:calib-metrics}

\subsubsection{Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and MAPE}

MAE measures absolute focal length error in pixels:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MAE}(f_x) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N |f_{x,\text{pred}}^{(i)} - f_{x,\text{gt}}^{(i)}|
\end{equation}
MAPE measures relative error as a percentage, enabling comparison across image resolutions:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MAPE}(f) = \frac{100}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{|f_{\text{pred}}^{(i)} - f_{\text{gt}}^{(i)}|}{|f_{\text{gt}}^{(i)}|}
\end{equation}

Both pose and calibration are benchmarked against ground truth. Pose is compared against AnyCam; calibration against both AnyCam and AnyCalib.

\section{Evaluation Datasets}\label{sec:datasets}

Three datasets provide ground truth for evaluation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MPI Sintel}~\parencite{butler2012sintel}: Synthetic animated sequences with exact ground truth poses, intrinsics, depth, and flow. Diverse scenes with dynamic content. Supports both pose and calibration evaluation.

    \item \textbf{TUM-RGBD}~\parencite{sturm2012tumrgbd}: Indoor sequences with motion-capture ground truth trajectories and known intrinsics. Supports both pose and calibration evaluation.

    \item \textbf{KITTI}~\parencite{geiger2012kitti}: Automotive driving sequences with GPS/IMU ground truth poses. Supports pose evaluation; calibration evaluation is discussed as a limitation in Section~\ref{sec:kitti-limitation}.
\end{itemize}

All evaluations use $N=200$ sequences (600 pose pairs) per dataset. Comparisons are on raw model outputs without post-optimisation (e.g., bundle adjustment).

\section{Training Convergence}\label{sec:training-convergence}

Training proceeded through all three phases on four datasets (approximately 82,000 frames, 77,000 training sequences). Training details (learning rates, batch sizes, loss formulation) are described in Chapter~\ref{chapter:solution-approach}.

\textbf{Phase A} converged within 5 epochs, with validation loss closely tracking training loss (Figure~\ref{fig:training-curves-a}).

\textbf{Phase B} training loss decreased steadily over 10 epochs (Figure~\ref{fig:training-curves-b}), confirming that MCT learns to aggregate spatial features effectively.

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{figures/training_loss_phase_b.png}
    \caption[Phase B training convergence]{Phase~B training loss (calibration reprojection against AnyCalib pseudo ground truth). Steady decrease from 19.6 to 2.4 over 10 epochs.}
    \label{fig:training-curves-b}

\textbf{Phase C} trained for 10 epochs; the epoch~5 checkpoint was selected based on validation performance. Flow reprojection loss decreased steadily, while the calibration anchor loss increased moderately---an expected behaviour as the model learns to deviate from AnyCalib predictions where doing so improves the self-supervised objective (Figure~\ref{fig:training-curves-c}).

A variant that additionally unfroze the DINOv2 ViT-L/14 backbone diverged during training, confirming that freezing the large backbones is necessary for stable convergence.

\subsection{Trajectory Visualisation}\label{sec:qualitative-results}

Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory-comparison} shows predicted trajectories on Sintel \texttt{market\_6} after Sim(3) alignment. Our method (ATE $= 0.176$\,m) follows ground truth more closely than AnyCam (ATE $= 0.352$\,m), a $2.0\times$ improvement. Across all 23 Sintel sequences, our method achieves lower ATE on 13 (57\%), with the largest gains on sequences featuring substantial camera translation through dynamic scenes. This highlights that there is room for improvement---one factor is that our model was trained with 4-frame windows, while AnyCam received progressive training at 2 and then 8 frames, suggesting that extending our training to longer sequences could yield further gains.

\subsection{KITTI Calibration Limitation}\label{sec:kitti-limitation}

While pose estimation improves on KITTI (Table~\ref{tab:pose-results}), calibration accuracy degrades compared to AnyCalib. KITTI's automotive cameras (long focal lengths, forward-facing motion) differ substantially from the training distribution. The MCT does not generalise its calibration to this out-of-distribution camera type. Extending training data to automotive scenarios would likely address this. Notably, pose estimation still improves on KITTI despite the calibration limitation, indicating that multi-frame consistency contributes independently.

\subsection{Ablation: Sequence Length}\label{sec:ablations}

The choice of $\text{max\_ahead}=3$ (4-frame windows) was validated in preliminary experiments (Appendix~\ref{chapter:preliminary-results}). Shorter sequences underutilise multi-frame information; longer sequences suffer from compounding flow composition errors. Full-scale results across all three datasets (Table~\ref{tab:pose-results}) confirm that this choice generalises well.
