\chapter{Future Work}\label{chapter:future-work}

This chapter discusses directions for extending the proposed framework.

\section{Progressive Sequence Length Training}

The current system trains on fixed 4-frame windows. The pose estimator used in our demonstration (AnyCam) was originally trained progressively on 2-frame and then 8-frame sequences. Adopting a similar progressive schedule could improve trajectory-level accuracy, as the current 57\% ATE win rate on Sintel suggests room for gains from longer temporal context.

\section{Scaling to Longer Sequences}

Beyond progressive training, the fixed window limits temporal context. Extensions include hierarchical flow composition ($O(\log N)$ depth), recurrent or streaming MCT variants for arbitrarily long sequences, and learned keyframe selection to maximise information per aggregation step.

\section{Broader Training Data}

The KITTI calibration limitation (Section~\ref{sec:kitti-limitation}) reveals that the MCT does not generalise to camera types underrepresented in training. Including automotive and other specialised datasets would broaden the framework's applicability.

\section{Alternative Pretrained Models}

The framework is not specific to AnyCam and AnyCalib. Future work could explore fusing different pose estimators (e.g., DUSt3R, DROID-SLAM) with different calibration networks, or extending the MCT to aggregate features from multiple complementary backbones simultaneously.

\section{Non-Pinhole Camera Models}

The current instantiation fits a pinhole model. Extending to fisheye or equirectangular cameras would require modifying the flow induction to operate on ray fields directly, fully exploiting the model-agnostic capabilities of per-pixel ray prediction.

\section{Joint Depth Refinement}

Depth predictions are currently treated as fixed inputs. Since depth and calibration are coupled through projection, joint refinement could yield further improvements, particularly where monocular depth estimates are unreliable.
