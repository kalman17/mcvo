\chapter{Future Work}\label{chapter:future-work}

This chapter discusses directions for extending the work presented in this thesis. While the proposed method demonstrates that multi-frame feature aggregation and staged self-supervised training can improve both calibration and pose estimation, several avenues remain for further development.

\section{Scaling to Longer Sequences}

The current system operates on fixed-length sequences of four frames ($\text{max\_ahead}=3$), a length chosen to balance multi-frame context against composed flow error accumulation (Section~\ref{sec:flow-composition}). A natural extension is to investigate architectures that can process longer sequences without the quadratic cost of direct flow computation. Possible approaches include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical flow composition}: Rather than composing all flows from a single reference frame, a hierarchical scheme could compose flows in a tree structure, limiting the maximum number of composition steps to $O(\log N)$ instead of $O(N)$.

    \item \textbf{Recurrent or streaming architectures}: Replacing the fixed-length MCT aggregation with a recurrent module that maintains a running state across frames would enable processing arbitrarily long sequences without requiring all frames to be present simultaneously.

    \item \textbf{Keyframe selection}: Instead of processing consecutive frames uniformly, a learned or heuristic keyframe selection strategy could identify the most informative frames for aggregation, maximising temporal coverage while minimising redundancy.
\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Pinhole Camera Models}

Although AnyCalib supports model-agnostic calibration through its per-pixel ray representation, this thesis fits a pinhole model to the predicted rays for compatibility with AnyCam's projection-based pose estimation. Extending the pipeline to non-pinhole camera models (e.g., fisheye, equirectangular) would require modifying the flow induction and reprojection steps to operate directly on ray fields rather than through an explicit intrinsic matrix. This would fully exploit AnyCalib's model-agnostic capabilities and broaden the range of supported camera types.

\section{Joint Depth Refinement}

The current pipeline treats depth predictions from UniDepth as fixed inputs. However, depth and calibration are coupled: errors in one affect the other through the projection equations. Joint refinement of depth, calibration, and pose---while significantly more complex in terms of optimisation---could yield further improvements, particularly in scenarios where UniDepth's monocular depth estimates are unreliable (e.g., textureless regions, reflective surfaces).
